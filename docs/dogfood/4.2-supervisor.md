## Outcome — ✅ the §6.4 worker-survives-and-is-reattached recovery landed as the contract requires

A throwaway `wkr-smoke` instance was created under the real `~/.forge`, `szork`
registered as its repo, a daemon started as a genuine background OS process
(java pid 56518). A workstream was created and a worker **spawned**: the daemon
provisioned an isolated clone of `szork` (`workers/w-1/checkout/`, carrying
`szork`'s committed HEAD `faa3635`) and launched a real `forge worker` JVM child
(pid 56979) that registered, drove the `adventure-gen-retry-config` feature from
`Drafting → InteractiveSpec`, and exported its feed. The daemon was then
**`kill -9`'d**, its state cache deleted, and a fresh `forge daemon start`
(java pid 58645) **reconciled the survivor from the log alone** and reattached.

`bootCount: 2`, and the full `w-1` record including both exported events — the
rebuild reconstructed *content*, not just counts, from the log (§6.4 invariant:
the log is canonical, the cache is a derivable convenience).

## What "reattach" means in 4.2 — liveness, not (yet) feed-resume

The reattach proven here is **liveness reattach**: on boot the daemon probes each
still-`live` worker's recorded pid (`ProcessHandle.of`), leaves an alive one
running, and watches it for exit (recording `worker.exited` — the `-1`
`ExternallyObservedExit` sentinel, since a bare `ProcessHandle` yields no real
code). That is the genuinely new and riskiest 4.2 mechanic — a worker *process*
outliving the daemon and being re-supervised — and it landed.

The worker's **event-feed resumption** is deliberately **not** completed in 4.2.
The worker's read-side feed exporter (`WorkerFeedExporter.follow`) phones each new
action home to the daemon socket; when the daemon dies that call fails and the
`foreverM` follow loop ends, so after the restart the daemon receives no *new*
exported events from the survivor (its `orch.run` continues regardless — the loop
is socket-independent, which is why the worker survives). The contract's full feed
reattach (§6.4(d): **durable worker-side offsets, replay from the last-acked
offset, no event lost or double-counted**) is coupled to the B2 budget-authority /
O6 credential-broker recovery handshake, both of which are **Slice 4.3**. A naive
4.2 resume (retry the follow loop) would be at-least-once with possible duplicates
at the crash boundary — exactly what the durable-offset protocol exists to
prevent — so it is correctly deferred rather than half-built. See
[`../design-4.2.md`](../design-4.2.md) §4 (carry-forward) and
[`../design-rationale.md`](../design-rationale.md).

## A real bug this run surfaced (and fixed)

The first attempt failed: the spawned worker died instantly with
`ClassNotFoundException: io.forge.app.Main`. `WorkerLauncher.Real` passed
`java.class.path` to the child verbatim, but `java -jar <relative-path>/forge.jar`
yields a **relative** classpath, and the worker child runs under
`cwd = workerRoot` (inside the instance dir) — so the relative jar was
unresolvable from the child's cwd. Fixed by absolutizing each classpath entry
against the daemon's cwd before launch (`WorkerLauncher.absoluteClasspath`, a
no-op for the already-absolute sbt-run classpath), with `WorkerLauncherSuite` as
the regression. This is precisely the class of fragility the "run code earlier /
capture real shapes" discipline expects a live dogfood to catch that a stubbed
launcher (`SupervisorSuite`) cannot.

## Relationship to the automated proof

`SupervisorReconcileSuite` (forge-app) is the in-process automated version: a real
child is spawned by a `RealSupervisor`, the supervisor + its `DaemonState` are
abandoned mid-life (the orphaned child survives), the cache is deleted, a fresh
`DaemonState` + `RealSupervisor` boot, and `reconcile` reattaches to the *same
still-alive pid* (then records its exit on kill via the rebound `ProcessHandle`
watcher). It composes the halves that `SupervisorSuite` (spawn/exit/reconcile, no
crash) and `DaemonCrashRecoverySuite` (log rebuild, record-only worker) each prove
separately. This dogfood is the matching real-`forge daemon` / real-`forge worker`
/ real-`kill -9` demonstration.
